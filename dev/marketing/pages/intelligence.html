THREE KINDS OF EVIDENCE

NOT EVERY MARKER
MEANS THE SAME THING

The same globe holds different kinds of information. Each has its own failure modes, and StratOps keeps them visibly apart.

Observed A sensor network saw it.

What it is
A position or measurement from a network: aircraft ADS-B messages, vessel AIS broadcasts, GNSS accuracy samples.
Where it breaks
Coverage gaps, delayed aggregation, stale or misleading broadcasts, ambiguous identity.
How StratOps shows it
Source, time of the last observation and identity confidence on the selected contact.

Reported A source said it.

What it is
A statement someone published: a naval announcement, a NOTAM, an incident report, a cyber advisory.
Where it breaks
Publication time, event time and location precision can all differ. Reports are corrected, repeated or withdrawn.
How StratOps shows it
The source link, publication date, status or expiry, and only the geometry the source supplied.

Predicted A model calculated it.

What it is
A calculation from inputs: satellite propagation, footprints, range overlays.
Where it breaks
Accuracy depends on the age of the inputs and on modeling assumptions. A calculation is not a sighting.
How StratOps shows it
Labeled as propagated or modeled where the data supports it, with the age of the input data.

ANATOMY OF A MARKER

FOUR THINGS TO CHECK
BEFORE YOU CONCLUDE

A

Source

What kind of evidence supports the item, when it was recorded, and which limitations apply. Available attribution is shown with the underlying record.

B

Time

Three clocks can differ: when it was observed, when it was published and when the underlying data was issued. Read the one that matches your question.

C

Identity

How confident the match is, and whether evidence conflicts. Conflicting registration or operator data can lower confidence.

D

Geometry

Whether the location is an exact point, a port, an area or an approximate notice radius. Precision is part of the evidence.

EVIDENCE BY DOMAIN

DATA TYPES VARY
BY DOMAIN

Each domain has different observation methods, update intervals and coverage limits. Read a layer according to what its evidence can actually support.

AIR

Aircraft observations

Aircraft observations are assessed alongside available identity evidence and timestamps. Military association depends on supporting details, not on a map symbol alone.

SEA

Vessels and naval publications

Observed vessel positions are kept separate from published descriptions of exercises, deployments and port activity. A location in a report is not a confirmed live ship position.

SPACE

Orbits and imagery

Satellite positions are computed from orbital data. Available imagery has its own acquisition time and should not be mistaken for a live view of a satellite or its mission.

AIRSPACE

Notices and status

Published airspace notices and operational status information are interpreted with their stated validity periods, geographic extent and any known uncertainty.

SIGNALS

GNSS degradation

Regional signal-degradation indicators are shown with observation periods, available sample context and coverage limits. A highlighted area does not identify a specific interference source.

EVENTS

Reporting and context

Reports, regional indicators and geographic reference context are presented with available dates, location precision and supporting evidence. Reported events are not live telemetry.

COMMON MISREADINGS

WHAT A SYMBOL
DOES NOT PROVE

A useful map distinguishes an observation from a claim or calculation.

You see It does not establish Check instead
A shaded GNSS cell A located jammer or confirmed spoofing. The observation period, sample information and coverage notes.
A port named in a naval publication A warship currently at that port. The publication date and whether the source supplied geometry.
No aircraft on the map That no aircraft is there. Receiver coverage, source access and broadcast gaps.
A satellite footprint Sensor coverage or a collection event. The orbital data age. The footprint is geometry only.
A 3D ship or aircraft model Identification, payload or mission. The identity confidence and the evidence behind it.
A base marker or range ring Current force presence, readiness or verified radar performance. The reference dataset’s date and its stated precision.
A vulnerability listing or threat report An active attack. The report’s category, date and source.
A thermal anomaly or seismic event A strike or an explosion. Whether an independent report corroborates it.
The same story across many outlets Independent confirmation. Whether the outlets trace back to one original source.
FRESHNESS

Each source keeps its own clock

Providers update at different intervals. Refreshing the page never creates a newer observation than the provider has published.

GAPS

Missing is not absent

Aircraft and vessels can be unobserved because of broadcast, receiver, access or classification gaps. The map shows what was received.

ACCESS

Rights and permissions vary

Some sources need credentials, licences or explicit enablement, and some features depend on your access.

Operational limitations

StratOps is an independent, public-source research and visualization tool. It is not a classified intelligence source, a complete worldwide surveillance feed, an official aviation advisory, a navigation authority or an emergency-warning system. Confirm consequential decisions with current authoritative sources.
