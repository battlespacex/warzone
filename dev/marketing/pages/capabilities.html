AIRPUBLIC-SOURCE

Military aviation

Follow the broadcast. Check the identity.

Aircraft observations come from several public ADS-B and aggregator networks. StratOps sets each one against identity references and a military taxonomy, so a contact arrives with its evidence attached, not just an icon.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Search and filter contacts, then focus a single track in 3D.
  • Read altitude, speed and heading next to the source and the age of the last observation.
  • Check identity confidence and any registration, operator or type fields. Ambiguous results stay ambiguous.
  • Open eligible history, replay a track, and return to the current picture.

Read it rightNo signal does not mean no aircraft. A gap in ADS-B coverage, or a military model on screen, says nothing about activity.

Aircraft observationsIdentity evidenceClassification confidence

SEAPUBLIC-SOURCE

Naval activity

Two layers. Never mixed.

The naval picture has two kinds of information that are easy to confuse. AIS contacts are positions that vessels broadcast. Publications are statements about exercises, deployments and port visits. StratOps keeps them in separate lanes.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Filter and select AIS contacts, then inspect details, contact age and eligible history.
  • Open the naval activity widget to browse publications by type, geography and date, with source links and expiry.
  • See a published point, port or area only where the source actually supplied one.
  • Review identity confidence and conflicts before a contact earns a specialized ship silhouette.

Read it rightA publication’s port or region is not a ship’s position, and an AIS ship-type code does not confirm a warship.

Vessel observationsPublished activityIdentity context

SPACEPUBLIC-SOURCE

Orbital intelligence

Predict the pass. Know the source.

Public orbital catalogs become moving objects. StratOps propagates published elements to show where military-associated and dual-use satellites are predicted to be, and keeps the age of that data in view.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Search the catalog and select a satellite by its NORAD identity.
  • Focus, follow and release a satellite, with orbital details and footprint where shown.
  • Trace predicted ground tracks computed from the published elements.
  • Keep imagery apart: observations have their own capture dates and sources.

Read it rightA position here is a prediction. A footprint is geometry, not measured sensor range, and a 3D model is a visual aid, not evidence of payload or mission.

Orbital predictionsGround tracksCatalog metadata

AIRSPACEPUBLIC-SOURCE

NOTAM & airspace

Find the restriction in space and time.

Notices are written for pilots, not for maps. StratOps keeps the original text and source, then draws the geography the notice actually supplies, so you can see where a restriction sits and when it applies.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Search and filter notices, then select one to focus its geometry.
  • Read the published text, source, status and effective period side by side.
  • Follow replacement and cancellation links between records.
  • Open Airspace Status for available airport delays and restrictions with their published details, and switch on maritime warning areas where enabled.

Read it rightQ-line locations and radii can be approximate, and a notice in one place is not a nationwide closure. For flight decisions, use the official authority.

Published noticesValidity windowsAirspace status

SIGNALSPUBLIC-SOURCE

GNSS interference

See where navigation degrades.

StratOps maps summaries of navigation-signal degradation as severity-colored cells. Regional patterns show up at a glance, and so do the limits of the data behind them.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Switch the layer on and choose a supported observation period.
  • Filter by geography and severity.
  • Hover or select a cell to read the data behind it, including source health and coverage.
  • Read the layer beside air tracks and notices for regional context.

Read it rightDegradation is not a located jammer and does not confirm spoofing. Rolling 24, 48 and 72-hour views can have partial coverage, and the layer reports it when they do.

Signal degradationRegional coverageObservation periods

EVENTSPUBLIC-SOURCE

Conflict intelligence

From an incident to a theater.

Reported events become a sourced, filterable layer. Strikes, drones and missile activity are placed by what the report says, with location precision shown, so each incident reads in the context of its region.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Toggle event categories and select a marker for its source, date, severity and available media.
  • Browse the Intel Wire and open article details with source links.
  • Check regional hotspots and event counts.
  • Review alert timing and areas where a supported alert feed is configured.

Read it rightA marker is a report, not telemetry. Syndicated repeats are not independent confirmation, and an animation is not radar detection.

Event reportsRegional contextAlert status

GEOSPATIALPUBLIC-SOURCE

Area of interest

Ask a question about a place.

Sometimes the subject is a place, not a platform. Draw an area and StratOps summarizes what is loaded in and around it, alongside reference context for strategic sites and environmental events.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Activate the tool, draw a polygon and adjust its vertices.
  • Scan for the aircraft, vessels, events and bases currently available in that area.
  • Read the summary, then reshape the area as the question changes.
  • Add reference layers: military bases, thermal and fire activity, seismic events.

Read it rightResults reflect loaded data, filters and location precision, and an empty scan is not proof of absence. A base marker or range ring is reference context, not current force presence.

Area summariesReference contextGeographic overlays

IMAGERYPUBLIC-SOURCE

Satellite observations

Know when the image was taken.

Imagery is evidence of a place at a time. StratOps links eligible image references to events and shows when each was acquired and where it came from.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Open eligible event media and source previews.
  • Read acquisition date, source and available metadata.
  • Keep image time separate from a satellite’s orbital position.

Read it rightAvailability depends on configuration and access. An image reference is context, not automatic confirmation of an attack or of a military object.

Image acquisition datesEvent-linked imagery

INFRAPUBLIC-SOURCE

Cyber & infrastructure

Add the infrastructure layer.

Cyber and infrastructure reporting joins the picture as sourced context: advisories, published threat intelligence and network-disruption observations, filed by category, time and supported location.

Explore detailed capabilities
  • Open the cyber status feed and browse reports by category, source and date.
  • Read geographic attribution and the evidence offered with each item.
  • Place internet-disruption observations next to regional events.

Read it rightA vulnerability listing or threat report is not an attack, and a traffic dip does not prove a countrywide incident. Some traffic metrics reflect billing country, not the target.

COMBINE LAYERS

ONE LAYER ASKS.
TWO LAYERS CHECK.

The strongest questions pair two kinds of evidence on the same geography, without treating one as proof of the other.

NOTAM×AIR

A restriction is published over a region. Which tracked aircraft were observed near it, and when?

Look for: timing. Compare the notice’s effective period with each track’s observation time.

GNSS×NOTAM

Does a degraded-navigation area overlap a published restriction?

Look for: overlap, not cause. Neither layer explains the other.

EVENTS×IMAGERY

Is there an eligible image reference for a reported event, and when was it acquired?

Look for: the gap between event time and acquisition time.

SEA×NOTAM

Does a published naval warning area contain any observed AIS contacts?

Look for: a warning polygon does not place a vessel inside it.
